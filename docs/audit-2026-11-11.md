# Whole-system audit, 11 Nov 2026

The owner asked for every component to be gone through: every bug, every thing that costs speed, everything that
should happen and does not. This is what was read, what was found, what was fixed and how each fix was checked, and
what is still open. Nothing here claims the system is perfect: the open list at the end is real.

**How it was done.** Every service's log on the server (recorder, copy job, qualify, discover, measure, book, events,
commands, strategist, operator, ops), the database's own tables (queue, verdicts, coverage), the agents' usage lines
(`operator/spend.jsonl`) and turn records (`operator/turns.jsonl`), and the code each finding pointed at. Every fix
has a test that failed first; the full suite was green before each of the three deploys (02:30, 02:50, 03:02 UTC).

## Still open

1. ~~Holes in the tape on every restart and every stream drop~~: built on 11 Nov (row 19) and seen on the server:
   six deploys and the 11-minute standstill of 07:12 (row 30) left no hole on the pump.fun side. **Still open: the

   recorder's processor share, and that the oldest row stops moving until 19 Nov. (This item first said the disk

3. **One command as root on the server** (`sudo bash /opt/snipetech/deploy/setup.sh`, the one run on 7 Nov) would
   make Ubuntu's own update job gentle (row 30: it stopped the machine for 11 minutes on 11 Nov and 13 on 7 Nov) and
   keep half the machine's memory for our services. Nothing breaks without it: the tape refills itself after such a
   stop and live trading runs at OKTech. Our services' memory limit is still hit constantly (723,000 events); the copy
   every three hours instead of every hour took away most of the cause.
4. **The history win-rate gate retires the wallet with the best paper numbers** (DEdEW3: +0.0264 SOL a copy, lower
   bound +0.0222 over 75 copies; retired because its own trades win 23.7 % of the time, under the 35 % floor). The gate
   is from the 7 Nov rules and stays until evidence says otherwise; a forward check on such wallets would settle it.
5. **What the agents cost is mostly the model and the cadence**: the fixes above take off the repeated part. Half the
   cadence halves the rest. That choice is the owner's.
6. **Failed duplicate sell transactions** (idea I7) cost on every live sell; changing that is a live setting.
7. **No live sniper has ever asked OKTech for preconfirmations** (row 27). The code has asked since 6 Nov; the
   server's settings file kept it off, and the newest snipers' stored requests carry no such flag. OKTech describes
   them as seeing the copied wallet's buy before it confirms, and approved the account for them. It changes how live
   buys are placed, so it is the owner's yes or no; with a yes the first 20 copies are compared with the 24 before.
8. **The wallets with the best paper numbers are all stopped by the crowding rule** (five with a lower bound of
   +0.02 to +0.04 SOL a 0.2 SOL copy; the one qualified wallet has +0.01). The rule rests on the live fills of

9. **Where the recorder's processor time goes** (rows 28-29): 2.9 ms a trade recorded, 0.1 ms of it the handling
   measured on the test machine. The work record's first readings will say; nothing is slow because of it (0.19 of
