# GMGN's query API: how it is called and what snipetech reads from it (12 Nov 2026)

## The answer first

**Reading does not need the destroyed private key.** A read is sent with the API key in one header, a timestamp and
a fresh id; nothing is signed. This comes from the source of GMGN's own command-line client (its `authExistRequest`)
and from GMGN's call-out page ("Read endpoints do not need `X-Signature`"). Confirmed on 12 Nov at 17:56 UTC: the
owner's Pro key answers from the server (`gmgn ping`: ok), and every read below was made with it through server jobs.
The key lives only on the server.

**The client was built from GMGN's documents and then checked against real answers the same day.** Seven things in
the documents were wrong or missing, and all seven are fixed and tested on the real answers. The section "Checked
against real answers, 12 Nov" lists them, with the fee unit first.

**Two reads are closed to us, and that is fine.** GMGN signs these with the private key, so our key cannot call them:

- a wallet's current holdings (`GET /v1/user/wallet_holdings`);
- the trades of the wallets our own GMGN account follows (`GET /v1/trade/follow_wallet`).

Everything else the owner asked for is open: the KOL and smart-money lists, a wallet's profit, win rate and history,
a coin's holders and traders with tags, trending coins, new pairs, candles, search by name. If holdings are ever
wanted, the owner would have to create a new key and keep its private half: do not do that for holdings alone, a
wallet's history (`wallet_activity`) answers most of it.

**GMGN never trades for us.** Every route that swaps, places or cancels an order, quotes a swap or creates a coin needs
the signature. `src/snipetech/gmgn.py` has no code for a signature and refuses any path that is not on its short list
of reads. The full map of GMGN's 19 skills, ranked by use, is `2026-11-12-gmgn-skills-map.md` (by a second helper);
this file is the HTTP side of it.

   Trimmed copies are the test answers in `tests/fixtures/gmgn/real/`.

## How a call is made

| part | value |
|---|---|
| host | `https://openapi.gmgn.ai` |
| key | header `X-APIKEY: <key>` (on the server: `GMGN_API_KEY` in `/etc/snipetech/env`) |
| every request | query `timestamp=<unix seconds>` and `client_id=<a new UUID>`, on a retry too |
| clock | the timestamp must be within 5 s of GMGN's clock (client source; the call-out page says 15 s) |
| replay | an id seen in the last 7 s is refused |
| body | JSON on the few POST reads, `Content-Type: application/json` |
| signature | none on a read. Signed routes add `X-Signature` (Ed25519 or RSA of path, query, body, timestamp) |
| network | IPv4 only. A 401 or 403 with a good key usually means the request left over IPv6 |
| answer | `{"code": 0, "message": "ok", "data": ...}`; on a failure `{"code": <http status>, "error": "<CODE>", "message": "..."}` |

The client binds its connection to an IPv4 address, so the server cannot leave over IPv6. The key was created for the
server's IPv4; from any other machine GMGN answers `AUTH_IP_BLOCKED`.

## Limits

- Plans, rate and burst in weight a second: Free 5, Plus 20, **Pro 50**. Counted for the key and its IP together, so
  every job on the server shares one budget. The header `X-RateLimit-Limit` names the plan's number; `gmgn ping`
  prints it (50 means Pro answered).
- A 429 carries `X-RateLimit-Reset` (unix seconds) or `reset_at` in the body. `RATE_LIMIT_BANNED` lasts about 5
  minutes, and **every request during a ban adds 5 s**, up to 5 minutes (`RATE_LIMIT_BANNED_MAX`).
- `403 PLAN_UPGRADE_REQUIRED`: the route weighs more than the plan holds. Never retried.
- What the client does: spends 10 weight a second (`[gmgn] weight_per_sec`), waits out a limit of under 10 s once,
  and after a longer one sends nothing until the reset time.
- The hourly discovery run costs about 160 weight (2 lists, the trending list, 5 coins' traders, the profit of all
  wallets in batches of 100, 40 wallets' statistics): about 3 seconds of the Pro budget, spread over 16 at our pace.

## Finding people: what is there and what is not

- **There:** the wallets on GMGN's KOL list and smart-money list, as their newest 200 trades each. The list is
  whoever traded lately, so polling builds the set (the discover stage asks once an hour). Each trade carries the
  wallet's X handle and tags.
- **There:** search by name or X handle (`gmgn who Cupsey`); the winners of any coin with their tags and names;
  per wallet the profit over 1, 7 and 30 days and all time, the win rate, the X followers and how many GMGN users
  follow it (`follow_count`: how copied it is).
- **Not there:** a global leaderboard of traders, a pump.fun leaderboard, a list of all KOL wallets without their
  trades. GMGN's site shows such boards; this API does not serve them.

## What was built

- `src/snipetech/gmgn.py`: the client, one method a read; `snipetech gmgn <action>` (`ping`, `kol`, `smart-money`,
  `top-traders [mint]`, `who <name>`, `wallet <address>`, `fees <address>`, `funder <address>`, `created <address>`,
  `token <mint>`, `trending`, `new-pairs`, `candles <mint>`, `gas`, `signals`, `callouts`; `--raw` prints GMGN's
  answer as it came).
- `discover run` has a source `gmgn`: at most once an hour (`[gmgn] every_hours`) it puts the wallets on both lists
  and the winners of the 5 hottest pump.fun coins into `candidates` with their numbers. Wallets GMGN tags as bots
  (`sandwich_bot`) are left out. **Naming is off by default** (`[gmgn] label = false`): GMGN's names change no group a
  lab reads until the owner switches it on. Switched on, a wallet GMGN names gets a label (kind `kol` for a KOL,
  `wallet` for any other named wallet, source `gmgn`), never over a label somebody else wrote.
- `doctor` has a row `gmgn`; the command is allowed as a server job and as an agent's job and holds no deploy back.
- **`snipetech tips`** (`src/snipetech/analysis/tips.py`, 12 Nov): the tip table, one line a followed wallet, from
  our own tape. See "The tip table" below.
- **`gmgn funder <wallet>`**: the wallet that funded it and the tracked wallets the same funder ties to it, as
  proposed companions. See "One operator's other wallets" below.
- **The wallet verdict says how followed a wallet is** ("followed by 2,147 GMGN users (GMGN tags: padre)"), as its
  last reason. No gate and no score reads it. See "How copied a wallet is" below.

## Checked against real answers, 12 Nov

Read with the owner's key between 17:56 and 18:45 UTC, about 35 look-ups in all. Every parser was put beside its real
answer. The tests in `tests/test_gmgn_real.py` run the client on those answers.

### 1. The unit of the fees: SOL, and GMGN's numbers are our tape's numbers

**`priority_fee` and `tip_fee` in a wallet's history are SOL, and they match our own record: 68 transactions of 68.**

First wallet, the one we track as Cupseyy (`6DQAGJ…`): 33 of its transactions of 12 Nov between 16:39 and 18:07 UTC
are both in GMGN's history and on our tape (`trades.priority`, `trades.tip`, read by signature).

- **17 transactions have one row on GMGN. All 17 match our tape to the lamport**, priority fee and tip both.
- **16 transactions have two rows on GMGN** (a coin bought with another token: one row for the coin, one for the
  token it was paid with). **Each row carries exactly half the transaction's fee. Added up, all 16 match our tape to
  the lamport.** Read row by row, such a trade looks half as expensive as it was.
- `gas_native` is priority fee plus tip plus 0.000005 SOL (what every signature costs), split over the rows the same
  way: 21 of 21 where the raw rows were kept.

Second wallet (`7PnR2C…`), to settle whether the tip is Jito's only. For 35 of its transactions of 12 Nov (17:41 to
17:51 UTC) we hold the full fingerprint, which names the service each tip went to: **all 35 tipped Helius Sender, not
Jito, and GMGN's `tip_fee` equals that tip in 35 of 35.** The priority fee matches in 35 of 35 too.

- **The tip is not only Jito's.** `tip_fee` is what the transaction sent to a landing service. Helius Sender is
  proven; Nozomi, Astralane and bloXroute were not seen, and neither was a transaction that tips two services.
- A trading app's own cut is not in it. On the first wallet's fingerprinted buys of 11 Nov the app took 0.001 SOL of
  a 0.099 SOL buy beside a 0.01 SOL tip; its tips on 12 Nov are 0.01 SOL in GMGN and on the tape alike.
- **Which one to trust: ours, and they agree.** Our tape reads the fee off the transaction itself. GMGN matched it
  68 times of 68 once a transaction's rows are added up. Use the tape for a fee study. Use GMGN for the wallets and
  the days the tape does not hold (bids are on the tape only since 11 Nov 16:34 UTC, and only for pump.fun coins).
- Two wallets, one afternoon. That is enough for the unit and for the split over rows. It is not a survey of
  every service.

What changed: `gmgn fees` adds a transaction's rows up and prints `priority_sol`, `tip_sol`, `bid_sol` (the two
together) and `gas_sol` per transaction, with the unit named. Its quartiles are over transactions. What this wallet
really pays to land a buy: **0.011 or 0.02 SOL** (priority fee 0.001 or 0.01, tip 0.01), 0.011 on a sale. The old
row-by-row reading said a median of 0.01.

The fee presets (`gmgn gas`) are SOL too: 0.001 / 0.005 / 0.01 for low / average / high, 0.0002 automatic, 0.001
with MEV protection. They are the same round amounts wallets' trades carry in the history.

`bought_fee` and `sold_fee` in the wallet statistics are **dollars**: everything the buys and the sales cost beside
the coins (network fee, tip and the exchange's cut). On 32 real buys the per-trade costs came to 1.86 % of the
dollars bought; the 7-day total is 1.89 %. They are printed as `buy_fees_usd` and `sell_fees_usd`.

## The tip table (`snipetech tips`)

`snipetech tips [--group big] [--wallet W ...] [--hours 30] [--gmgn] [--json]`. A research job like `place-bids`,
allowed for the server's agents, and a deploy waits for it.

For each followed wallet, from our own tape: at each of its own first buys of a coin, the outsiders in its block
(its companions and our own wallets left out). One line a wallet:

- how many of its first buys had an outsider in the block;
- what the first outsider bid: median, 75th and 90th percentile;
- the bid that was above every outsider in 50, 75 and 90 % of those blocks;
- a suggested bid, one step (0.0001 SOL) above the 50 and the 90, and how often that bid would have been the
  highest on the same blocks;
- how many different wallets stood behind it, and its three most regular copiers with their median bid.

With `--gmgn`: how many GMGN users follow each wallet, and what its two most regular copiers pay by GMGN's history
(the median `bid_sol` of their buys). Six requests to GMGN a run at most (`--lookups`); every answer is kept a day.

**Outbidding is not landing first.** The chain orders by fee per compute unit and by who is there first. The
playbook found the block's highest bidder first in 56 % of blocks (725 of 1,292). The table prints that sentence and
the same share measured on its own blocks. A wallet with fewer than 5 blocks gets no suggestion. Blocks without an
outsider are not in the percentiles. A bid belongs in the tip: a tip is paid once and comes back on a failed send.

Not yet run on the real tape (it is not deployed). Its tests use a made-up market; `place-bids --each` reads the
same blocks and has been run on the server.

## How copied a wallet is (the wallet verdict)

GMGN's `follow_count` and tags are stored with a candidate's stats when the discovery source asked for its
statistics, and kept when any look-up asks (`gmgn wallet`, `gmgn funder`, `tips --gmgn`). The qualify stage puts one
more reason on the finished verdict, last: "followed by 2,147 GMGN users (GMGN tags: kol, padre); information only:
no gate and no score reads it". The same numbers go into the wallet's score record under `gmgn`.

**No status, score or review date changes.** A test runs the stage twice on the same five wallets, with and without
GMGN's numbers, and compares every field of every verdict. A rule on it ("too followed to copy", "never a tagged
bot") needs the owner's word first.

## One operator's other wallets (`gmgn funder`)

`snipetech gmgn funder <wallet>` prints the wallet that funded it (GMGN's `fund_from_address`, with the amount and
the day) and the wallets we track that the same funder ties to it: the funder itself, the wallets it also funded, the
wallets this wallet funded. It looks in three places: kept GMGN look-ups, the discovery source's rows, and our own
funder record. One look-up of weight 3, or yesterday's kept answer.

They are **proposed** companions. The proposal is printed and stored (`gmgn:proposed_companions:<wallet>`) and
nothing reads it. A lab scores a copy behind a wallet's companions as the tape finds them (`analysis/companions.py`),
and that rule is unchanged. Beside each proposal the command says what the tape's rule says of that wallet today
(`tape_says_companion`). A funder that is a service (GMGN names it, or our labels do) proposes nobody: an exchange
funds thousands of wallets.
