# Buy nothing new; bid inside the slot

**Bottom line: there is nothing you physically need to buy today.** Your own measurements settle the first question: on seven live copy fills OKTech put you in the leader's own slot (the ~400 ms block) six times out of seven, and you still finished 2–4 trades behind him at an 18–43 % worse price because the order inside a slot is a fee auction — the bots directly behind the leader paid 0.01–0.3 SOL per fill while you paid the 0.001 SOL minimum and landed last every time ([live-recipe.md](docs/live-recipe.md), "Inside the slot"). No stream, landing service, hosting box or rival executor fixes that; only a bigger bid per fill (and a fill size that makes the bid affordable) does, and OKTech already exposes that knob per task. The paid-fee test you have queued (0.2 SOL fills, 0.02 SOL priority + 0.02 SOL tip against a minimum-fee control, five fills each) is the correct next spend and costs SOL on-chain, not a subscription. On the second question — finding profitable wallets that few bots copy — the bottleneck is sample size (the one survivor, `G2oZrd…`, has n = 37 and earns about 0.6 SOL a day at 0.2 SOL a trade; [roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md)), and the fix is a wider candidate net over a longer window: Vybe's free 30-day whole-market board today, and one optional €50/month purchase (SolanaTracker Advanced, the only self-serve 90-day ranking of every wallet). Everything else surveyed — raw shred feeds at $1,000/IP, Nozomi/0slot/NextBlock/bloXroute senders, bare-metal hosting at $296–2,900/month, Dune, Cielo, Bitquery, Moralis, a second executor — is either redundant with what you own (Helius Business, LaserStream, Birdeye Starter, OKTech, a staked-routing API, a second trading API) or only matters if you one day build your own sender, which the roadmap correctly calls a different business. Two corrections to earlier assumptions: Flipside is defunct (data business sold to SonarX on 19 May 2026, domain parked), Jito ShredStream shut down on 5 September 2026, and Birdeye's own pricing page says Starter is **3 M** compute units a month, not the 8 M in `docs/birdeye.md` — check your dashboard before relying on the daily budget.

## The slot is won by the bid, not the pipe

The system's own tape is the most important evidence in this report because it is the only slot-level measurement of a hosted copy bot that exists anywhere; the researchers found no independent benchmark of Trojan, GMGN, Axiom, Bloom, Nova or Banana Gun copies ([Dysnix, 5 Nov 2026](https://dysnix.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot)). Reading the transactions ahead of your seven fills, the picture is consistent: the leader's own wallets sit adjacent with identical fees (unbeatable), then bots paying **0.033 SOL Jito tip on a 2.47 SOL buy**, **0.200 SOL priority fee to sit directly behind Decu on a 4.94 SOL buy**, **0.325 SOL tip (8 % of a 4.15 SOL buy)**, then you at position 392, 270 and 461 of the slot ([live-recipe.md](docs/live-recipe.md)). For a bot buying 1–5 SOL, 0.01–0.03 SOL of fees is 1–3 % of the trade; for your 0.05 SOL fills it was 20–60 %. The copy test failed on bid and size, not on speed.

The scheduler facts explain why the bid works and why it is not a guarantee. The Solana leader drains a queue ordered by reward per compute unit, so a higher price **tends** to execute earlier: one post-Alpenglow study of 49 mainnet slots found a 0.74–0.89 rank correlation between fee and position for uncontended transactions, with about 18 % inversions when transactions arrive in the same 50 ms window ([DEV Community empirical study](https://dev.to/duongynhi000005oss/inside-the-block-how-solana-validators-actually-order-transactions-post-alpenglow-empirical-study-5b0i)). Jito's BAM sequencer, which held 27–32 % of stake in early 2026 by Jito's and Blockworks' figures and 60 % by Triton's May 2026 count, orders by fee per compute unit inside a 50 ms tick, first-in-first-out among transactions touching the same account ([Blockworks, 17 Feb 2026](https://app.blockworks.com/report/solana-s-block-building-wars?from=bwr); [Triton, 5 May 2026](https://blog.triton.one/swqos-for-everyone-why-we-retired-cascade-marketplace/)). Nothing in the protocol enforces fee ordering ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-local-fee-markets)), and a higher bid can only beat what is still unscheduled when it arrives — it cannot reorder entries already written. Paying more buys a better place among the bots that arrive in the same tens of milliseconds, which is exactly the crowd you lost to.

Two free levers come out of this. First, the **requested compute-unit limit dilutes priority**: Agave's formula is `(priority fee × requested CUs + base fee) / (1 + requested CUs)`, so a pump.fun buy left at a 200,000 CU limit while using ~22,000–70,000 pays for up to 6× more priority than it gets ([Anza](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/introducing-the-central-scheduler-an-optional-feature-of-agave-v1-18); [empirical study](https://dev.to/duongynhi000005oss/inside-the-block-how-solana-validators-actually-order-transactions-post-alpenglow-empirical-study-5b0i)). OKTech's `extraParams.computeUnits` is the knob; set it tight before the paid-fee test so the priority fee is not wasted ([oktech-settings.md](docs/oktech-settings.md)). Second, the **network-wide Jito tip floor is irrelevant**: live at 08:44 UTC on 6 Nov 2026 the 50th percentile landed tip was 2,387 lamports and the 99th 76,471 lamports (0.00008 SOL) ([bundles.jito.wtf tip_floor](https://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_floor)), two to four orders of magnitude below the 0.03–0.325 SOL you observed. Nozomi says it plainly: the tip you need "scales with how contended the accounts you touch are" ([Temporal/Nozomi FAQ](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq)). Quoting any vendor's tip percentile tells you nothing about a coin a known leader just bought.

What the bid cannot buy is a place inside the leader's own bundle, and a true "backrun" (your buy packed right behind his in one Jito bundle) needs his signed transaction before it is in a block, which no public feed provides since Jito closed its mempool in March 2024 and ShredStream in September 2026 ([docs.jito.wtf](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/); [Jito feed docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/)). That matches your roadmap's conclusion that the create-slot regime is out of reach. The honest economics of the paid-fee test are therefore: a 0.04 SOL bid on a 0.2 SOL fill is 20 % of size, so the test can prove **position** (did we move from last to second?) but cannot by itself be profitable; if position moves, the follow-up question is whether the +1 edge the lab measured (about +0.04 SOL per 0.2 SOL trade across the top wallets, [roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md)) survives at a fill size where a 0.03 SOL bid is 2–3 %, i.e. 1–1.5 SOL, where your own price impact is larger than the paper assumed. That is unknown and is the thing worth spending SOL to learn.

## Finding uncrowded wallets needs a wider net, not a new vendor

Your wallet labs are starved of sample, not of tooling. At the +6 landing (where live copies actually fill) 34 of 132 wallets are positive over 32 hours of tape, 11 with a positive lower bound, and exactly one clears n ≥ 20 at both landings ([roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md)); the 271-wallet "quiet follow-through" hunt found only creators and outliers ([live-recipe.md](docs/live-recipe.md)). Two things widen the net cheaply: a longer window than the tape has lived, and a whole-market ranking rather than per-token or per-wallet lookups. Only three self-serve APIs expose a pre-computed market-wide wallet leaderboard. **SolanaTracker** `/v2/pnl/leaderboard/top` ranks the top 500,000 wallets per period over 1/7/30/90 days, sortable by realised PnL, ROI, win percentage or trade count, for **€50 a month** (Advanced, 200,000 requests; free tier 2,500 requests at 3/s) ([SolanaTracker leaderboards](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pnl-v2/leaderboards.md); [pricing](https://docs.solanatracker.io/pricing.md)). **Vybe** `/v4/wallets/top-traders` does 1/7/30 days with win rate and trade counts, up to 1,000 rows a page, on the **free** plan (25,000 credits, 60 requests a minute) ([Vybe reference](https://docs.vybenetwork.com/reference/get_top_traders_v4); [Vybe plans](https://docs.vybenetwork.com/docs/plans-rate-limits)). **GMGN**'s board is reachable only through a reverse-engineered endpoint, but it is the one source with a `follow_count` — how many GMGN users copy a wallet — which is the closest external proxy for "crowded" that exists ([Apify GMGN scraper](https://apify.com/parsebird/gmgn-copytrade-wallet-scraper.md)); its official Agent API is whitelist-only and swap-oriented ([GMGN docs](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/gmgn-agent-api.md)).

The crowding measure you actually trust — the price gap between a wallet's buy and the next buy after it, and the count of trades between — needs slot and transaction-index data, and only three sources expose it: Helius `getTransactionsForAddress` (`slot` + `transactionIndex`, 1,000 transactions a call, 10 credits per 100, unlimited mainnet retention; [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/rpc/gettransactionsforaddress)), Bitquery (`Block_Slot`, `Transaction_Index`, archive add-on from ~$70; [Bitquery](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/examples/Solana/Pump-Fun-API/)) and Moralis swaps (`transactionIndex`, but no Solana PnL product; [Moralis](https://docs.moralis.com/web3-data-api/solana/reference/get-swaps-by-wallet-address)). SolanaTracker, Vybe and Cielo give millisecond timestamps derived from block time, identical for every trade in a slot, so they cannot break ties ([SolanaTracker wallet trades](https://docs.solanatracker.io/data-api/wallet/get-wallet-trades.md)). You already own the best of these: your Business plan's 100 M credits make backfilling a candidate's 90-day history (10 credits per 100 transactions) essentially free, and your tape records block position for every pump.fun trade going forward. So the pipeline is rank with SolanaTracker/Vybe, gate with Birdeye's 30-day summary, verify position and crowding with Helius history and the tape, then the +1/+6 labs — no new vendor in the chain except the €50 board.

**The Birdeye conflict.** `docs/birdeye.md` states Starter = 8 M CU a month at $99 with $12 per extra million; Birdeye's own pricing page (read through a search snippet because the docs host was mid-migration with many 404s) says Starter $99 = **3,000,000 CU**, 15 requests/s, **$33 per extra 1 M**, and Premium $199 = 10 M CU, 50 rps, $9.9 per extra million ([Birdeye pricing](https://docs.birdeye.so/docs/pricing)). If the page is right, your daily budget is ~100 k CU, not 266 k, and overage is nearly three times dearer than planned. Birdeye also cannot rank the market: its top-traders endpoint is per token (now to 90 days, sortable by realised PnL; [Birdeye changelog](https://data.birdeye.so/docs/changelog/20260418-release-top-trader-improvements-for-solana)), which is why it is a gate, not a net. **Flipside** is not an option: Studio and API were sunset in mid-2025, the data business was sold to SonarX on 19 May 2026, and on 6 Nov 2026 the domain served a parking page ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/913502316/flipside-sells-blockchain-data-business-to-sonarx-commits-fully-to-edisyl-enterprise-ai-platform)).

## Conclusion

The tool market in November 2026 sells speed, and speed is the one thing you have already proved you have. What decides a pump.fun copy is a per-coin auction measured in hundredths of a SOL that the vendors' tip-floor charts do not even register, and the only lever that enters it is money bid per fill through the executor you already use. That turns the "what do I buy" question into a sizing question: the paid-fee test tells you whether 0.02–0.04 SOL moves you from last to second, and if it does, the real decision is whether to trade at 1–1.5 SOL per fill where such a bid is 2–3 % of size, with price impact the paper never modelled. Until that is measured, every subscription in this report is a distraction with a monthly fee.

On the wallet side the implication is patience with a slightly wider net. The one survivor at +6 earns a few tenths of a SOL a day, and no external dataset will reveal an uncrowded winner your tape cannot see; a 90-day board and free 30-day board simply propose more candidates for the same three gates, and Helius history lets you check their crowding before the tape has aged. If a month of that produces nothing with n ≥ 20 and a positive lower bound at +6, the honest reading is that the copy edge on these wallets has been arbitraged by the bots paying 0.03–0.3 SOL a fill — in which case the next thing to buy is still not a tool, but a different entry.
