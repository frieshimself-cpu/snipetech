# Unblock the Claude agents before December

For one person with a Gmail address, Anthropic offers no self-serve button that reliably lifts the Start tier's **$500 monthly cap** before 1 December 2026. The fastest legitimate way to keep Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 running is to send traffic through a provider that Anthropic's spend cap does not cover. In order of speed, those are OpenRouter (minutes, about 5.5 % dearer), Google's Vertex AI (large default quotas, Google bills), Microsoft Foundry (list price, Azure bills) and, more slowly, Amazon Bedrock. Anthropic's own routes are the right long-term fix but they are slow or uncertain. Tiers move up automatically on unpublished criteria. Buying credits does nothing to the tier. Max and Ultra credits only reach Start. A Team plan needs a business email and two real members. Support "can also raise limits", but users in 2026 describe its AI bot blocking escalation. One fact from this repo sets the stakes: the agents spent the $500 monthly cap in about two weeks of November (roughly $35 a day on average), and at full speed they cost about $8–10 an hour (up to about $200–240 a day; the owner's ceiling is $500 a day). So the 1 December reset buys about two weeks at the November pace, and a Build-tier lift ($1,000 a month) about a month at that pace but only four or five days at full speed. A daily budget set in the agents' own code decides which of these it is. The ranked plan at the end fixes today and works on the cap in parallel.

## Anthropic's own ladder has no fast rung for a Gmail individual

The official mechanism is short. "Organizations are placed on a tier automatically based on usage history and account standing and can move to a higher tier over time as they use the API." Once the cap is hit, "API usage pauses until 00:00 UTC on the first day of the next month, unless you request a higher limit sooner." The API then returns a 429 with `enforced_spend_limit_reached` and no `retry-after` header ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). The caps are **Start $500, Build $1,000, Scale $200,000**, and Custom has no cap ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). Anthropic publishes **no dollar, time or invoice threshold** for moving from Start to Build. Its help center says tiers are assigned automatically and that no deposit or purchase moves an organization up ([support.claude.com 10366389](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10366389-how-can-i-get-higher-rate-limits-on-the-claude-api)). The only published hint about what counts comes from Claude Platform on AWS, whose organizations move up "as they build a history of paid AWS Marketplace invoices" ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). By analogy, a few paid months on the Console probably matter, but that is a guess. No user report of a Start→Build timeline under the system introduced on 26 June 2026 turned up. Any advice about "deposit $40 to reach Tier 2" describes the retired Tier 1–4 scheme ([aifreeapi](https://www.aifreeapi.com/en/posts/claude-api-quota-tiers-limits)).

The documented self-serve step is **Request tier increase** on the Console's Rate limits page, and the same sentence adds that "Anthropic support can also raise limits" ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). The help center sets one condition: the organization must be using at least 50 % of its current limits ([support.claude.com 8114527](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114527-i-m-encountering-429-errors-and-i-m-worried-my-rate-limit-is-too-low-what-should-i-do)). An organization sitting at 100 % of its cap clearly meets it.

### The Gmail rejection has no published rule behind it

No Anthropic page found requires a business or custom-domain email for the tier form, for the Console or for contacting sales. No Reddit, Hacker News or GitHub post was found reporting that the form rejects Gmail, and no exact error text was found. The only "no Gmail" rule Anthropic publishes belongs to the **Claude Team plan** in claude.ai: "Public domains like @gmail.com, @yahoo.com, and @hotmail.com aren't allowed" for the account creator ([support.claude.com 9267247](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9267247-get-started-with-the-team-plan)). The reported rejection is therefore real for this person but unexplained. Three explanations fit the evidence. The button may lead to a sales-style lead form that screens free-mail domains. The rule may be an unpublished one. Or it may be a bug. A further reason not to trust any single 429 blindly: on **7 October 2026** Anthropic confirmed that "some organizations [were] incorrectly paused for having reached their spend limit" ([MIXED News](https://mixed-news.com/en/anthropic-spend-limit-incorrectly-paused-organisations-october-7/)).

The legitimate workarounds are the following. First, screenshot the exact rejection. Then open the Console's **Get help** menu as the organization's Admin or Owner. Only Console Admins reach the human Product Support team; Fin, the AI bot, answers first and can hand the case on ([support.claude.com 9015913](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9015913-how-to-get-support)). The one published checklist for a limit request comes from the AWS page: name the models, the peak input and output tokens per minute for each, and the share of input that is cached ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). Add the organization ID, the `enforced_spend_limit_reached` error, the form's refusal and the cap you need.

Changing the login to a domain the owner genuinely owns, or adding a real colleague with a domain address as Admin, might get the form submitted. Nothing documented says it changes the decision, because limits are set at the organization level on "usage history and account standing" ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). No user report either way was found. Inventing a company or a member to pass a screen is not a workaround: the Usage Policy bars intentionally bypassing "restrictions, or guardrails established within our products" ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)).

Set expectations for support low. No response time is published ("Response times vary by plan and by the severity of the issue") and there is no phone line ([support.claude.com 9015913](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9015913-how-to-get-support)). The 2026 reports found are all about billing, not limits. In them Fin replies "Escalation isn't available for this conversation", and tickets are closed automatically ([anthropics/claude-code #57719](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/57719); [vibegraveyard.ai](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/anthropic-fin-support-escalation-loops/)). In one April 2026 case a user waited over a month ([nickvecchioni.github.io](https://nickvecchioni.github.io/thoughts/2026/04/08/anthropic-support-doesnt-exist/)). No report was found of support raising a spend cap, quickly or otherwise.

## Paying Anthropic more does not move the cap

Prepaid credits, auto-reload and plan credits all fail for the same reason: the cap follows the tier, not the balance. Auto-reload "purchases additional credits automatically when your balance falls below a threshold", and the help article links credits to no tier ([support.claude.com 8977456](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8977456-how-do-i-pay-for-my-api-usage)). Monthly invoicing in arrears exists, but only "through an invoicing arrangement" with Sales ([support.claude.com 8977456](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8977456-how-do-i-pay-for-my-api-usage)).

Subscription credits move a linked Console organization only at the margin. "If their monthly credits are over $200 USD, they move up to at least the Build tier"; otherwise the plan lifts the organization only to Start, and the credits "don't count toward moving to a higher tier" ([API credits for Max, Ultra, and Team plans](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)). That rule sorts the plans as follows.

| Plan | Price | Monthly API credit | Tier effect | Fit for this person |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max | $100/mo | $100 | Start only | Adds $100 of usage, which counts toward the same $500 cap |
| Ultra | $200/mo | $200 | Start only (exactly $200 is not "over $200"; worth confirming) | Same |
| Team | Seats from $20 (Standard) and $100 (Premium) annual, $25/$125 monthly ([pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)) | $20 / $100 per seat, pooled, capped at $500 | At least Build if over $200 | Blocked: Gmail cannot create it, two or more members required, "not intended for individual use" ([9267247](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9267247-get-started-with-the-team-plan)) |

Credits are claimable only "after 7 days on an eligible plan" ([API credits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)). The cheapest Team mix whose credits go over $200 costs about $220 a month on annual billing. It would be at least a week before Build, and Build only doubles the cap to $1,000. One help-center summary states the Team→Build rule without the $200 condition ([support.claude.com 10366389](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10366389-how-can-i-get-higher-rate-limits-on-the-claude-api)), which conflicts with the docs. Either way, Team is not open to a solo Gmail user.

## Extra organizations add risk, not headroom

Workspaces cannot add spend: "Organization-wide limits always apply, even if workspace limits add up to more" ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). A second self-made Console organization is not named as forbidden anywhere found. Holding several organizations, such as a personal one and a work one, is normal ([akitaonrails/ai-usagebar #599](https://github.com/akitaonrails/ai-usagebar/issues/599)). But creating one *to get a second $500* sits close to two Usage Policy lines. One bars bypassing product restrictions. The other bars creating "multiple accounts, to avoid detection" ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). New organizations "may start in the Evaluation tier", below Start, and "account standing" feeds tier placement ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). A cap-splitting second organization therefore probably starts lower and could damage the standing that the main organization needs to move up. No suspension for this was found, and no ruling either.

**Claude Platform on AWS** is the same trap in AWS packaging. Signing up "always provisions a new Anthropic organization", placed on Start with "the same monthly spend caps". It has no Request tier increase button; the page sends you to an account representative. The setup form asks for an "entity type", and the docs do not say whether "individual" is offered ([Claude Platform on AWS](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-platform-on-aws); [Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). It moves up only on paid AWS invoices, which are issued monthly in arrears, so the first lift is at least a month away. **Claude Models on Google Cloud (Anthropic managed)** likewise creates "a new Anthropic organization", and higher limits there go through an account representative or sales ([Anthropic managed](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-models-on-google-cloud/anthropic-managed)). Neither is a route around the problem.

## Cloud partners and routers carry no Anthropic spend cap

The real escape is a provider that bills the usage itself. Anthropic documents spend caps only for its own API and Claude Platform on AWS. The Bedrock and Google-managed pages describe quotas set by the cloud provider and no monthly cap ([Rate limits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits); [Claude in Amazon Bedrock](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock)). The reference price on the Anthropic API is Fable 5.2 at **$8 / $40** per million input/output tokens, Opus 5.5 at **$4 / $20** and Sonnet 5.5 at **$2 / $10** ([Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

| Route | All three models? | Anthropic $ cap | Default throughput | Price vs list | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OpenRouter | Opus 5.5 confirmed; Fable 5.2 and Sonnet 5.5 not checked | None | Not published | +5.5 % credit fee | A third party sees every prompt |
| Vertex AI (Google managed) | Yes | None | Opus 5.5: 20M input / 2M output TPM; Sonnet 5.5: 25M / 2.5M (global) | Google-set; multi-region +10 % | "access might be restricted" on some accounts |
| Microsoft Foundry | Yes (Fable 5.2 only "Hosted on Anthropic") | Not documented | Not published | List price (CCU) | Subscription eligibility and quotas unknown |
| Amazon Bedrock | Yes (Fable 5.2 only on bedrock-runtime) | None | Mantle 2M input TPM; new accounts often at 0 | Global probably list; regional +10 % | Days to weeks for a new account |

**OpenRouter** is the quickest. It lists Opus 5.5 with Anthropic, Bedrock, Vertex, Azure and Claude Platform on AWS behind it, "with no markup on the provider's price" ([OpenRouter Opus 5.5](https://openrouter.ai/anthropic/claude-opus-5.5)). It charges a **5.5 % fee when buying credits** ($0.80 minimum), keeps "zero logging" of prompts by default, and publishes no paid rate limits ([OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)). Bringing your own Anthropic key would bill the capped organization, so pay OpenRouter directly. Two things are unverified: whether Fable 5.2 and Sonnet 5.5 are listed, and whether prompt caching and Anthropic beta features survive when requests rotate across providers. No 2026 user report confirms high-volume use after an Anthropic cap.

**Vertex AI** has the best published numbers. It serves `claude-fable-5-2`, `claude-opus-5-5` and `claude-sonnet-5-5` under the same IDs as Anthropic ([Google managed](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-models-on-google-cloud/google-managed)). Its default global quota is ten times the Start tier's 2M input tokens per minute for Opus and Sonnet. Google warns that "maximum quotas might vary by account and, in some cases, access might be restricted", and gives Fable 5.2 only "Granted upon model enablement" with no numbers ([Google quotas](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/partner-models/claude/quotas)). It supports prompt caching and basic web search. It lacks Batches, the Files API, code execution and web fetch ([Google managed](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-models-on-google-cloud/google-managed)). Google's per-token prices were not retrieved.

**Microsoft Foundry** bills through the Azure Marketplace at Anthropic's standard rates and needs only "an active Azure subscription" and a Foundry role ([Claude in Microsoft Foundry](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-microsoft-foundry); [Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Default quotas, setup time, and whether free-trial subscriptions can buy the offer are all undocumented. It is also undocumented whether "Hosted on Anthropic" deployments, the only option for Fable 5.2, carry any Anthropic-side tier.

**Amazon Bedrock** looks good on paper and is slow in practice. Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 sit on the `bedrock-mantle` Messages API with a 2M input-TPM default, which can be raised to 5M input / 500k output without Anthropic's approval. Fable 5.2 runs only on `bedrock-runtime`, and access to all three is gated per model ([Claude in Amazon Bedrock](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock); [bedrock-runtime](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-on-amazon-bedrock-legacy)). On bedrock-runtime, Fable 5.2 output tokens count **10x** against quota, and `max_tokens` is reserved up front ([AWS token burndown](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/quotas-token-burndown.html)). The biggest problem is new accounts. Several re:Post threads report Claude daily quotas stuck at **0** and "Not adjustable", with support cases unassigned for days ([re:Post](https://repost.aws/questions/QUxr781LoTQjuuxJiRwy_YkQ/all-bedrock-token-per-day-quotas-stuck-at-applied-value-0-not-adjustable-on-new-account-support-case-unassigned-for-2-days)). One write-up waited about a month for the defaults ([bitcreed.us](https://bitcreed.us/notes/aws-bedrock-accessdeniedexception-claude-zero-quota)). AWS says increases favour "customers who generate traffic that consumes their existing quota allocation" ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/jp_jp/bedrock/latest/userguide/quotas-increase.html)).

A caution for unattended agents applies to all four: none of these routes has Anthropic's hard monthly stop. The cap that blocked the agents was also their spending brake. Whatever replaces it needs a budget limit set in the agents' own code or the cloud account.

## Ranked plan for this owner

**1. Today: put the agents on OpenRouter behind a model-name switch.** Check that Fable 5.2 and Sonnet 5.5 are listed, and buy a modest block of credits. Run one research round to confirm that caching and the agents' features still work and that spend matches the estimate. It costs about 5.5 % extra and takes minutes. Because the strategy prompts are about a live trading edge, decide consciously that a router seeing them is acceptable. The agents' own $500-a-day budget must become the hard stop, because OpenRouter will not stop them.

**2. This week: open a Google Cloud project with billing and enable Claude on Vertex AI.** If the account gets the published default quotas, move Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 there for 10x the Start throughput and no Anthropic cap. Request a Fable 5.2 quota at enablement, and keep OpenRouter as a fallback until Vertex has carried a full day. Microsoft Foundry is the equivalent second choice at exact list price if Google restricts the account.

**3. In parallel: ask Anthropic properly, once.** As Admin, use Console **Get help**, attach the screenshot of the form's rejection, and ask a human to raise the tier. Give the organization ID, the error code, the models, the peak tokens per minute, the cached share and a realistic monthly spend: about $1,000–1,500 at the November pace, more at full speed, which is a Build figure at least. Treat a reply as a bonus. Once 1 December arrives, keep the Console organization as a paid-history builder for automatic advancement, but not as the agents' only route.

**4. Later, only if the first three fail: Bedrock.** Open the AWS account now, so verification and model access age in the background, but do not count on it this month.

**Do not:** create a second Console organization or a Claude Platform on AWS organization to double the cap, invent a company or colleague to pass the email screen, buy credits or a Max or Ultra plan hoping to change the tier, or wait for 1 December as the plan.

## Conclusion

The question was framed as getting a bigger Anthropic cap, but the owner's own budget turns it into choosing a provider. A Start-tier organization is built to hold $500 a month while Anthropic builds trust in it, and these agents spent that in about two weeks. Even a successful Build request would only about double their running time. The durable answer is to make the model provider swappable: Vertex AI or Foundry as the main source, OpenRouter as the instant fallback, and the Anthropic organization ageing toward Build and Scale on its own clock.

Two facts remain unknown and could reorder the ranking: why the tier form refuses this Gmail address, and whether a new Google or Azure account gets the published quotas. Both are cheap to learn. A screenshot answers the first, and one enablement attempt answers the second.
