Scope note: everything below was collected on 2026-11-06. Items dated before mid-2026 are flagged "(pre-mid-2026, possibly stale)". ## Key Question 1 — Streaming sources: what exists, what it costs, and latency from on-chain inclusion to delivery (by provider / region)

### Takeaway
In November 2026 the market has three layers: (a) raw shreds over UDP (DoubleZero Edge, Helius Raw Shreds at $1,000/IP/mo, Everstake Blockspace, ERPC Direct Shreds at €398–798/mo, Shyft RabbitStream bundled in $199+ plans) which deliver a pump.fun buy ~1–15 ms (Frankfurt, DoubleZero-path vs non-DZ) to ~120–270 ms (shred-fed vs plain Yellowstone, older RPC Fast number) ahead of a Geyser gRPC feed; (b) Geyser/Yellowstone gRPC (Helius LaserStream in 9 regions on the $499 Business plan and up, QuickNode included on $499 Scale / $999 Business, Shyft $199–649, Triton pay-as-you-go, RPC Fast $49–549) delivering at "processed" commitment, i.e. within the ~400 ms slot plus tens of ms of network/queue delay; (c) WebSocket / Kafka layers (Helius Enhanced WSS, Bitquery Kafka <500 ms p95, Bitquery WebSocket ~1 s) that are too slow for same-slot reaction. Jito ShredStream, the previous default raw-shred source, was shut down on 2026-09-05.

### Inferences
- For a pump.fun copy-trader the realistic latency ladder, from the leader's tx entering a block, is: raw shreds on a DoubleZero-connected box in Frankfurt (sub-10 ms claims) < raw shreds over public internet in the same city (≈ +1–15 ms per RPC Fast's DZ-vs-non-DZ Frankfurt test) < Geyser gRPC at `processed` in the same region (tens of ms after the block is replayed by the provider's node, i.e. typically 50–200 ms after the leader included it, per the Elite Node ranges and Helius' 28–89 ms created_at-to-receipt samples) < WebSocket/Kafka (300 ms–1 s+).
- No provider publishes a "pump.fun buy event on-chain → delivered" figure per region; the only way to get a trustworthy number for your box is Helius' Method 1 (two parallel streams, compare BlockMeta receipt) or RPC Fast's Geyserbench run from your own server for ≥1 hour.
- Cheapest credible gRPC path with Frankfurt/Amsterdam endpoints: Shyft Build $199 (includes shred-derived RabbitStream) or Helius Business $499 (includes LaserStream in 9 regions). QuickNode is only economical if you already need its $499 Scale plan.

### Gaps
- No independent, cross-provider, same-box benchmark of "slot inclusion → event delivered" in ms for 2026 was found; every number is vendor-published.
- Everstake Blockspace pricing page (Connect/Ultra prices) returned 404; prices unknown.
- Triton Dragon's Mouth gRPC list price not published; third-party figures conflict.
- Helius does not say whether its raw shreds come from its own validators or via DoubleZero Edge, nor which cities the UDP feed originates in.
- Bitquery's gRPC latency ("sub-300ms") has no disclosed methodology.

## Key Question 2 — Does ShredStream give enough head start to react within the same slot? Cost and access in 2026

### Takeaway
Jito ShredStream no longer exists: it was shut down on 2026-09-05 and Jito pointed users to DoubleZero Edge (permissionless, paid in USDC per device per epoch, ~$30–100/epoch by city as of a May-2026 price list). Shreds do arrive before the slot is finalized by the leader's replay, but the measured advantage of a raw-shred feed over a good co-located gRPC feed is single-digit to low-double-digit milliseconds on the DoubleZero path and tens-to-hundreds of ms on the public internet — enough to get a reaction tx into the *next* slot (400 ms) reliably, but no source demonstrates landing a copy trade in the *same* slot as the leader's buy, which would additionally require the leader to be the current/next slot leader you are connected to and your tx to reach it inside the remaining slot time.

### Inferences
- "Same-slot reaction" is not demonstrated anywhere. The best public evidence (RPC Fast Frankfurt) shows a shred feed beats a non-DZ shred feed by ~1–15 ms and a shred feed beats a non-shred gRPC feed by ~120–270 ms (older data). Given a 400 ms slot and ~100–150 ms of slot already elapsed by the time a leader's tx is shredded and propagated, a Frankfurt box on raw shreds plausibly has ~200 ms to decode, decide, build, sign and deliver to the *current or next* leader; practically that means next-slot inclusion is the realistic target, same-slot only when the box is physically near the current leader and the leader is also the next leader (Solana leaders hold 4 consecutive slots, so this is not rare).
- With Jito ShredStream gone, the three ways to get raw shreds are: DoubleZero Edge directly (cheapest at scale, needs a DZ-connected host — Cherry Servers FRA/AMS/LT/SE advertise DZ interconnection), a reseller UDP feed (Helius $1,000/IP, ERPC €398–798, Everstake Connect/Ultra), or Shyft's RabbitStream (shreds pre-decoded with gRPC-style filters, bundled in $199+).
- For a research tape (recording every pump.fun trade) raw shreds add engineering cost (reconstruction, dedup, >90% but <99% reliability per ERPC) with little analytic benefit; gRPC at `processed` is the right recorder source, shreds matter only for the sender's trigger path.

### Gaps

- Current DoubleZero Edge per-city price list (post-May-2026) not retrieved; official DZ docs did not appear in search results.
- No source quantifies how often a copy tx lands in the same slot as the leader's tx under any feed.

## Key Question 3 — Hosting: regions/providers that co-locate with Solana leaders and Jito block engines; bare metal vs VPS; cost; is a 4-vCPU VPS a bottleneck?

### Takeaway
Stake (and hence leader slots) is concentrated with three bare-metal hosts — TeraSwitch (26.7%), Latitude.sh (16%), Cherry Servers (14%) — and geographically in Germany/Netherlands (Frankfurt, Amsterdam) plus US East; Jito's block engines sit in Amsterdam, Dublin, Frankfurt, London, New York, Salt Lake City, Singapore and Tokyo. A Frankfurt or Amsterdam bare-metal box at one of those three hosts (or any DoubleZero-connected host) is the default choice for the sender; for a 24/7 gRPC recorder writing several GB/day plus hourly CPU-heavy replays, a $300–560/mo small bare-metal (Latitude m4/f4.metal.small-medium, Hetzner AX42/AX102 at €79–259 in Falkenstein/Nuremberg) is cheaper and more predictable than a 4-vCPU VPS; no source benchmarks a 4-vCPU VPS for this workload, but Helius' own numbers (10 MB/s for all transactions, 2x headroom) and Yellowstone's operations guidance (dedicate cores, decouple I/O from decode, avoid single-thread backlog) imply 4 shared vCPUs is marginal once replays run concurrently with the tape.

### Inferences
- Sender box: Frankfurt first, Amsterdam second. Both have a Jito block engine, Helius LaserStream + Sender endpoints, Shyft/ERPC/Everstake shred endpoints, the two largest European validator clusters, and (Cherry Servers) DoubleZero connectivity. New York/Newark is the US equivalent (Jito NY, Helius ewr, Shyft NY/Ashburn); Tokyo has endpoints but the smallest leader share. Choose the host by availability: Latitude.sh and Cherry Servers publish prices and are themselves top-3 stake hosts, so a box there shares a building or metro with a large fraction of leaders; TeraSwitch (largest) sells quote-only.
- Best price/performance for the 24/7 recorder + hourly replays: Hetzner AX102 (16c Zen4 X3D, 128 GB, 2x1.92 TB NVMe, €159/mo) in Falkenstein/Nuremberg if ~5–10 ms extra to Frankfurt is acceptable for a *recorder* (it is: tape fidelity does not depend on being first), or Latitude f4.metal.medium ($555) / m4.metal.small ($296) if it must sit in the same city as the sender. These give dedicated cores for SQLite writes and replays without stealing from the gRPC consumer thread.
- Latency-critical sender: a small dedicated box in Frankfurt or Amsterdam (Latitude f4.metal.small $398 or m4.metal.small $296; Cherry Servers custom small config; Vultr Frankfurt bare metal ~$295–350 if the listings are accurate) is sufficient — the sender does little CPU work; what matters is being on a 10 Gbps non-virtualized NIC in the right metro and, ideally, DoubleZero-reachable.
- 4-vCPU VPS verdict: not formally benchmarked anywhere. From the cited guidance: a full-transaction gRPC subscription is ~10 MB/s of protobuf to decode (one busy core), SQLite batching plus fsync is another, and hourly CPU-heavy replays will saturate the remaining two shared vCPUs; with hypervisor steal and the "single consumer thread backlog → disconnect" failure mode, a 4-vCPU VPS will likely drop the stream during replays. It is workable only if the recorder filters server-side to pump.fun/PumpSwap program IDs (an order of magnitude less data) and replays are niced/throttled. For the sender alone a 2–4 vCPU VPS in Frankfurt is acceptable if jitter is tolerable, but a dedicated small metal box costs about the same ($296–398 vs $100–200 for a premium 4-vCPU VPS) and removes the jitter.

## Key Question 4 — Helius plan tiers in 2026, what a "$600/month" plan buys, LaserStream regions/inclusion, and whether dedicated nodes matter for sending vs reading

### Takeaway
Helius' current ladder is Free $0 / Developer $49 / Business $499 / Professional $999 / Enterprise custom, with LaserStream gRPC mainnet included from Business up (9 regions, no per-region surcharge), raw shreds sold separately at $1,000 per IP per month ($800 on Professional), data add-ons only on Professional, and dedicated nodes "starting from $2,900/mo". There is no $600 tier: ~$600/month most plausibly means Business ($499) plus overage/credit top-ups, or a legacy plan. Dedicated nodes buy you gRPC with no shared-tenant throttling, Jito bundle simulation and colocation — i.e. they matter for *reading* at volume and for predictable stream latency; for *sending*, Helius' shared plans already give staked connections (Developer+), Sender with 7 regional endpoints at 50 TPS default, and 50–100 sendTransaction/s on Business/Professional, so a dedicated node is not required to land transactions.
