# Solana transaction landing and in-slot ordering for copy trading (as of 6 Nov 2026)

Scope note: research date 2026-11-06. Anything dated before mid-2026 is flagged "(pre-mid-2026, possibly stale)". Live numbers (Jito tip floor) were pulled from the API at 08:44 UTC on 2026-11-06. Internal evidence from this repo's own live test log (`docs/live-recipe.md`) is cited by file path, not URL.

## Key question 1 — Inside one slot, does a higher compute-unit price reliably place a transaction before lower-priced ones that arrived earlier? What does the 2026 scheduler actually do?

### Takeaway
On Agave (the default since v2.3 is the `central-scheduler-greedy`; the older `central-scheduler` was removed in v4.1) and on Firedancer's pack tile, the leader drains a priority queue ordered by reward per compute unit, so a higher CU price *tends* to execute earlier within the leader's window — one empirical post-Alpenglow study found Spearman 0.74–0.89 between CU price and intra-slot position for uncontended transactions, but with ~18 % inversions among transactions arriving in the same 50 ms window, and on contended (hot writable account) transactions the chain serialises so ordering is much looser. Nothing enforces strict fee ordering at the protocol level; arrival time still matters because the queue is drained continuously and a later-arriving higher bid can only beat what is still unscheduled. On BAM (Jito's TEE sequencer, ~27–32 % of stake in early 2026 by Blockworks/Jito figures, 60 % per Triton in May 2026) ordering is explicitly priority-fee-per-CU within a 50 ms tick, with FIFO among conflicting transactions.

### Cited Findings
- Agave priority formula since v1.18 central scheduler: `Priority = ((priority fee × requested CUs) + base fee) / (1 + requested CUs)`; requested CU limit therefore dilutes priority — [Anza, Introducing the Central Scheduler](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/introducing-the-central-scheduler-an-optional-feature-of-agave-v1-18) (pre-mid-2026); same formula restated in [Bauer/Rezabek/Carle, forum.solana.com, 18 May 2026](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803).
- Agave changelog: `central-scheduler-greedy` added in v2.2 ("much better performance than the more strict central-scheduler"), made default in v2.3, `central-scheduler` deprecated in v4.0 and "no longer supported" in v4.1 (falls back to greedy); `--enable-scheduler-bindings` (external schedulers over IPC) added in v4.0; scheduler-bindings bumped to v4 in 4.1 and v5 in 4.4; UDP TPU ingestion removed (QUIC only) in v4.0 — [anza-xyz/agave CHANGELOG.md (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/CHANGELOG.md).
- Greedy scheduler behaviour: transactions sanitized, prioritized into a priority queue; worker threads get CU budgets; the highest-priority transaction is scheduled immediately; if it conflicts with the current batch, the batch is sent and a new one started; the central (now removed) scheduler instead built a dependency DAG and held back conflicting high-priority transactions — [forum.solana.com overview, May 2026](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803).
- Firedancer pack tile: priority = reward / compute resources; treaps for pending txs, votes, penalties (hot-account writers go to a penalty treap) and pending bundles; bundles are FIFO within the bundle treap and never mixed with votes; "transactions in a microblock can execute in any order"; modes: Balanced (default; votes always scheduled, bundles on one bank tile, other txs paced) and Perf; a deprecated "bundle mode" scheduled single transactions only in the final 50 ms of a slot — [forum.solana.com overview, May 2026](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803); reward-to-compute ordering also in the [Firedancer block-packing internals mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/internals/block-packing) (third-party mirror; page returned 410 on re-fetch).
- Empirical post-Alpenglow study (49 mainnet slots 464182838–464182886, posted 1 Nov [2026]): Spearman rank correlation between CU price and intra-slot position 0.74–0.89 across 4 non-vote banking threads for uncontended txs; 18 % of pairs inverted when a lighter batch on another thread flushed to PoH 1–3 entries ahead of a higher-fee tx arriving in the same 50 ms window; leaving a 200 000 CU limit on a ~22 000 CU swap dilutes priority "up to 6×" under write-lock contention — [DEV Community, duongynhi000005oss](https://dev.to/duongynhi000005oss/inside-the-block-how-solana-validators-actually-order-transactions-post-alpenglow-empirical-study-5b0i). Caveats: small sample, no tx count or CI, self-published.
- Helius: "Neither Solana's consensus nor the scheduler enforces strict transaction ordering based on priority fees" — [Helius, The Truth about Solana Local Fee Markets](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-local-fee-markets) (pre-mid-2026, possibly stale, but the protocol statement still holds: no SIMD enforces ordering).
- SIMD discussion #605 proposes making priority ordering within each entry batch a block-validity rule; a commenter notes Firedancer entries hold one transaction each so it would not bind Firedancer — [solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents discussion 605](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/discussions/605) (status: discussion, not adopted as of the search).

### Inferences
- "Higher CU price beats earlier arrival" is true only for what is still in the queue when your transaction arrives. Within a leader's 400 ms slot the scheduler is draining continuously, so a transaction that arrives 30–50 ms after the leader's buy competes only against transactions not yet flushed; the leader's own transaction (and anything in the same or earlier flush) is already in PoH. Paying more raises your rank among the *contemporaneous* backlog; it cannot reorder past entries.
- On a hot pump.fun bonding curve all copy buys write-lock the same curve account, so they serialize on one thread (Agave) or in the penalty treap (Firedancer). Within that serialized chain the greedy scheduler takes the highest priority first, which is why observed in-slot order on a contested coin looks like a fee auction (see Q4 internal evidence) rather than arrival order — but the 18 % multi-thread inversion rate and FIFO-on-conflict in BAM mean fee is not a deterministic lever.
- Requested CU limit matters as much as CU price: a pump.fun buy set to 200k CU but using ~60–70k CU pays for ~3× the priority it gets under Agave's formula and Firedancer's reward/compute ratio. Set CU limit tight.
- Which scheduler you face depends on the leader: Agave greedy (minority of stake), Jito-Solana/BAM (majority), Firedancer/FireBAM. Expect per-leader variance in how much a fee buys.

## Key question 2 — Can a Jito bundle land in the same slot immediately after a specific target transaction (backrun), and what tip percentile is needed in late 2026 for pump.fun activity?

### Takeaway
A Jito bundle only guarantees order *inside the bundle*; to sit directly behind a specific target transaction you must include that signed target transaction in your bundle (the classic frontrun/victim/backrun shape), which requires seeing it before it is in a block — Jito shut its mempool in March 2024 and is shutting ShredStream (which only shows transactions already in a block) on 5 Sept 2026, so there is no public pre-inclusion feed of other people's pump.fun buys. Without the target tx in your bundle, "same slot after the target" is probabilistic: it depends on the leader being Jito/BAM, on you reaching the block engine while the slot is still open, and on your tip in a 50 ms auction. The live Jito tip floor on 2026-11-06 08:44 UTC was tiny (p50 ≈ 2.4e-6 SOL, p95 ≈ 2.4e-5 SOL, p99 ≈ 7.6e-5 SOL), i.e. network-wide tips are not what decides pump.fun ordering; the contested-account auction is.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A true "backrun the leader in the same slot" via bundle is not available to a copy bot in Nov 2026 because the leader's transaction is not observable before inclusion. What is achievable is "land in the same slot, somewhere after the leader", by reacting to the first shred/preconfirmation carrying the leader's buy while the same leader still has the rest of the slot (and up to 3 more consecutive slots) open.
- Because the global tip floor is in the thousands of lamports, quoting p75/p95 of the Jito floor is irrelevant for pump.fun contests; the effective "percentile" is set by the handful of bots competing for the same curve account. The internal evidence in Q4 (0.03–0.325 SOL tips / 0.2 SOL priority fees on single fills) is two to four orders of magnitude above the network p99.
- Using `sendTransaction` with `bundleOnly=true` (1-tx bundle) gives revert protection but puts you in the 50 ms auction; a plain `sendTransaction` to the block engine or a SWQoS path skips the auction wait, which Helius and Nozomi both argue can be faster.

### Gaps
- No 2026 measurement was found of how often a non-bundled copy transaction lands in the same slot *and after* the target versus a slot later, as a function of tip. Only vendor testimonials (Nozomi "median 0 slots", RPC Fast "same slot or +1") exist.
- DoubleZero Edge pricing and latency were not researched (out of the listed sources).
- The claim that BAM preconfirmations (Helius `preconfSubscribe`) expose other people's transactions pre-shred is from Helius docs; whether it is early enough to bundle against was not evidenced.

## Key question 3 — Landing services and their 2026 cost model, latency claims, regions, staked connections, API form, minimum commitments

### Takeaway
By late 2026 almost every sender converged on the same shape: a mandatory SOL tip instruction (0.001 SOL minimum at Jito-less "SWQoS pools" like 0slot, NextBlock, Nozomi, bloXroute, Astralane Free and Helius Sender Max; 0.000005 SOL on Helius SWQoS-only; 0.00001–0.0001 SOL on Astralane VIP tiers), plus either a monthly subscription (NextBlock $249/$749/$1 749 for 5/20/50 TPS; bloXroute "Build Your Own" $250/month per 150 credits/min) or pure pay-per-landed-tx (Nozomi, Helius, 0slot's new "free" tiers, Astralane). Triton stopped charging for staked sending in Feb 2026. All run Frankfurt and Amsterdam endpoints; measured RTT from an Amsterdam box to each provider's Amsterdam endpoint was 0.05–0.56 ms in June 2026, so provider proximity is not the bottleneck.

## Key question 4 — What do professional pump.fun copy bots use and pay (2026 evidence)?

### Takeaway
Public 2026 evidence on what winning pump.fun copy bots pay is thin and mostly vendor marketing (recommending 0.0005–0.01 SOL tips), which conflicts with on-chain observation: this repo's own 6 Nov 2026 read of seven copy fills found bots directly behind the leader paying 0.03–0.325 SOL Jito tips or 0.2 SOL priority fees on 2–5 SOL buys, while a 0.001 SOL bid landed last in the slot every time. Infrastructure vendors describe the production stack as Yellowstone gRPC / shreds for detection (5–40 ms), pre-signed templates, calibrated priority fee, multi-relay multi-region submission, and aim for "same slot or +1".

### Cited Findings
- Internal observation (this repo, 6 Nov 2026 08:30 UTC): on seven copy fills, buyers between the leader and our fill paid e.g. 0.033 SOL Jito tip (2.47 SOL buy, position 334), 0.200 SOL priority fee to sit directly behind the leader (4.94 SOL buy, position 25), 0.325 SOL Jito tip (4.15 SOL buy, "8 % of the buy"), 0.032 SOL priority + 0.003 tip directly behind the leader; three bots at 0.0004–0.010 SOL; our 0.001 SOL priority fill landed last (positions 392, 270, 461); "we were in the leader's slot six times out of seven… The order inside the slot is a fee auction: the leader's own wallets are unbeatable, then bots paying 0.01–0.3 SOL per fill, then us at the 0.001 minimum, last every time" — `/home/user/snipetech/docs/live-recipe.md` (section "Inside the slot: what the buyers ahead of us paid"). Earlier in the same log: live copies paid 18–43 % above the leader's price at a −7 % mean per fill.
- Vendor defaults in open-source copy bots: Jito tip default 0.0005 SOL, recommended 0.0005–0.001 SOL — [Jigsou/pumpfun-copy-trading-bot](https://github.com/Jigsou/pumpfun-copy-trading-bot); a pump.fun API guide lists 0.005 SOL minimum viable and 0.01 SOL standard Jito tip — [pumpdevio Medium](https://medium.com/@pumpdevio/pump-fun-api-atomic-token-launches-with-jito-bundles-f60c2fc6be3e); another vendor says priority 0.0005–0.002 SOL and Jito tips 0.001–0.05 SOL and that a pump.fun round trip costs 3–6 % all-in — [memegateway fee comparison 2026](https://memegateway.com/academy/solana-trading-bot-fees-compared-2026/) (vendor content).

### Inferences
- Vendor "0.0005–0.01 SOL" guidance describes uncontested flow; on a coin a known leader just bought, the realised auction is 0.01–0.3 SOL per fill among 1–5 SOL buyers, i.e. 1–3 % of their size. Matching that on a 0.05 SOL fill is 20–60 % of size, which is why bid size and position size must scale together.
- The professional pattern inferred from the internal fills is "same slot, directly behind the leader, bought with a single large priority fee or Jito tip", not a bundle backrun.
- Since priority fee goes to the leader regardless of client while Jito tips only work on Jito/BAM leaders, bots paying 0.2 SOL in priority fee (not tip) are hedging across schedulers.

## Key question 5 — Realistic latency budget from seeing the leader's buy in a shred/gRPC stream to a landed buy in the same slot, from Frankfurt/Amsterdam/NY

### Takeaway
Same-slot landing is physically feasible from Frankfurt/Amsterdam/New York because a slot is ~400 ms and each leader holds 4 consecutive slots, while shred/preconf detection is 5–40 ms after the leader executes and provider hops are sub-millisecond; vendors (Nozomi "median 0 slots", RPC Fast "same slot or +1", Helius "zero-slot") and this repo's log (in the leader's slot 6/7 times) agree on feasibility. The binding constraint is not reaching the slot but the position inside it: by the time your transaction arrives (≈40–100 ms after the leader's buy) the scheduler has already flushed entries, and your place among the remaining contenders for the same curve account is a fee auction.

### Cited Findings
- Detection: Yellowstone gRPC 10–40 ms; gRPC + ShredStream 5–20 ms; detect-to-submit budget "under 60 ms"; "See the target's swap in 15 ms and land your mirror in 45 more, and you copy their edge" — [RPC Fast playbook, Jul/Oct 2026](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-build-a-solana-copy-trading-bot) (vendor).
- Helius Preprocessed Transactions arrive up to ~8 ms before `processed`; Preconfirmations arrive before shreds ("the instant the leader executes them" on Helius leaders, "when the validator commits to executing them" on BAM); LaserStream "surfaces notifications while a slot is being executed, not after it finishes", so a reactive tx "could land in the same slot"; preflight simulation adds "upwards of 100ms" so use `skipPreflight: true, maxRetries: 0` — [Helius zero-slot blog](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot); [Helius Shred Delivery](https://www.helius.dev/docs/shred-delivery.md); [Helius trading overview](https://www.helius.dev/docs/trading/overview.md).
- Helius advises measuring latency from the same region as the endpoint because network distance dominates cross-continent tests — [Helius LaserStream measuring latency guide](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/laserstream/guides/measuring-latency).
- Shred feed comparison in Frankfurt (vendor benchmark, 10 014 txs): OrbitFlare first 95.93 % of the time; when Jito's feed was behind it was behind by 6.51 ms median and 28.77 ms p99; OrbitFlare regions Frankfurt, Amsterdam, London, New York, Singapore, Dublin, Siauliai, Salt Lake City, Tokyo — [OrbitFlare, DEV Community, Jul 2026](https://dev.to/orbitflarerpc/jito-shredstreams-last-slot-your-two-ways-out-53np) (vendor).
- Jito block-engine proximity: ERPC Frankfurt ping to the Jito block engine 0.2 ms (down from 0.3 ms); same-network block-engine connection newly available in Frankfurt and Amsterdam (previously only NY and Tokyo) — [ERPC Frankfurt restock, Jul 2025](https://erpc.global/en/news/2025/07/31/erpc-frankfurt-ryzen-metal-shredstream-restock/) (pre-mid-2026, possibly stale). Jito's older ShredStream docs listed average slot-completion speedups ~46 ms Amsterdam, 45 ms Frankfurt, 28 ms NY, 25 ms Tokyo — [Jito ShredStream gitbook](https://jito-labs.gitbook.io/mev/searcher-services/shredstream) (pre-mid-2026, service shutting down 5 Sep 2026).
- Provider hop from an Amsterdam server: 0.046–0.561 ms RTT (see Q3 table) — [Arct Cloud, Jun 2026](https://www.arct.cloud/blog/solana-trading-vps).
- Jito auctions tick every 50 ms; BAM also uses a 50 ms tick — [docs.jito.wtf](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/); [Blockworks Feb 2026](https://app.blockworks.com/report/solana-s-block-building-wars?from=bwr). Agave paces scheduler budget in 25 ms intervals (Oct 2026 PR) — [agave PR 16449](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/pull/16449).
- Leader geography matters: Frankfurt/Amsterdam for EU leaders, Ashburn/NY for US East, Tokyo for APAC; multi-region submission recommended — [RPC Fast sniper guide 2026](https://rpcfast.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot) (vendor). bloXroute and Astralane both recommend sending to the closest region and the global endpoint in parallel — [bloXroute Regions](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/trader-api/introduction/regions.md); [Astralane Endpoints](https://astralane.gitbook.io/docs/low-latency/endpoints-and-configs.md).
- Alpenglow activated on mainnet October 2026 (150 ms finality per vendor write-up); Frankendancer dropped — [bam.dev/docs](https://bam.dev/docs); [RPC Fast, 27 Oct 2026](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-build-a-solana-copy-trading-bot) (links an Alpenglow article). Slot duration figures in 2026 sources still cite ~400 ms — [memegateway](https://memegateway.com/academy/solana-trading-bot-fees-compared-2026/) (vendor).
- Internal: "we were in the leader's slot six times out of seven — OKTech's speed is not the problem" — `/home/user/snipetech/docs/live-recipe.md`.

### Inferences
- Budget from an EU box: leader executes buy at t0 → shred/preconf at Helius/OrbitFlare/DoubleZero edge ≈ t0+5–20 ms (same continent as leader) or +60–90 ms if the leader is in the US (transatlantic ≈70–80 ms one way) → decode/build/sign <1 ms with pre-built templates → sender hop ≤1 ms → provider to leader: ≤10 ms same region, 70–90 ms transatlantic → leader's queue. Same-slot is routine when leader and bot share a continent; for a US leader seen from Frankfurt the round trip alone consumes ~150 ms of the 400 ms slot, so same-slot still works if the leader's buy was early in the slot, otherwise you land in the next slot of the same leader (slots 2–4 of its rotation) or the next leader.
- Running two boxes (Frankfurt/Amsterdam + NY) and firing from whichever is nearer the current leader (leader schedule is known per epoch; validator IPs via gossip) removes the transatlantic penalty; this is what "multi-region submission" means in vendor copy.
- Because the leader keeps flushing entries every few ms, arriving 40 ms after the target typically puts you tens-to-hundreds of entries behind it (internal positions 25–461 in the slot); position inside the slot is then bought with fees, consistent with Q1 and Q4.
